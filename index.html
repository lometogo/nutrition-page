Two things to know before you configure

The RAM tax is the story of 2026

up to +$7,500

A worldwide shortage of memory and flash — driven by AI data-center demand — pushed Apple into its broadest price increase in years on 25 June 2026. Every Mac rose, and memory upgrade prices in some cases doubled.

MacBook Neo+$100$599 → $699
MacBook Air+$200entry now $1,299
MacBook Pro+$300Max tiers +$500–600
iMac 24"+$200entry now $1,499
Mac mini+$200on the previous M4 Pro
Mac Studio+$1,300biggest jump, M3 Ultra

The result is that the upgrade grid, not the base machine, now decides your bill. On a Mac Studio, going from 96 GB to 256 GB of unified memory adds $4,000; going from 1 TB to 16 TB of storage adds $7,500. Those two ticks alone cost more than the whole computer — and a 512 GB Studio, the largest memory Apple has ever shipped, arrives in late October 2026.

Practical rule: buy the RAM you'll need in three years. Buy the storage you need next year — external SSDs are far cheaper per terabyte.

Goodbye Mac Pro

The Mac Pro was discontinued in March 2026. Its product page now redirects to the general Mac hub, and the lineup listed for sale is exactly the six machines on this page.

The practical consequence: there is no longer a Mac with internal PCIe slots or user-expandable storage. If your work needs capture cards, fibre cards or big internal RAID, that now means Thunderbolt enclosures hanging off a Mac Studio, which is now Apple's flagship desktop. Apple's own answer is Thunderbolt 5 and an external PCIe chassis.

It also makes the ladder simpler than it has been in a decade: one laptop family per tier, one consumer desktop, one small desktop, one big desktop.

How the numbers were built

Starting prices are the current U.S. list prices read directly from Apple's online store on 21 October 2026. "Fully loaded" means that model's most expensive orderable configuration: top chip bin, maximum unified memory, maximum SSD, and — where offered — nano-texture glass. Prices exclude tax, AppleCare+, and software. Apple changes configurations without notice, so treat every figure as approximate and confirm at checkout.
